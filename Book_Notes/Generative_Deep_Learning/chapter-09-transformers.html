Detailed Notes

GPT and the wine-review dataset

Source: book pp. 235-237 / PDF p. 263

The chapter trains a small decoder-only GPT on wine reviews. Prepare consistent review boundaries and tokenization, create shifted input/target sequences, and choose a vocabulary and maximum sequence length suited to the data.

The model learns continuation, not a database of objectively verified wine properties. Generated reviews should be checked for repetition, contradictions, and copying. A small project clarifies architecture without implying the capabilities of a frontier-scale pretrained model.

Attention, queries, keys, and values

Source: book pp. 238-240 / PDF p. 266

Attention builds a context-dependent representation by weighting other token representations. Queries express the information sought, keys determine matching, and values provide the content combined.

Q=XWQ, K=XWK, V=XWV
Attention = softmax(QKT/√dk)V

Scale dot products to avoid extreme logits as dimensions grow. Apply softmax over the intended key/token dimension. Tensor axes for batch, heads, positions, and features must be explicit; swapping sequence and feature dimensions can produce a runnable but incorrect model.

Multihead attention and causal masking

Source: book pp. 241-244 / PDF p. 269

Multiple heads learn complementary projections and attention patterns; concatenate/project their outputs back to the model width. Causal masking prevents a token representation from accessing later target positions.

Set disallowed attention logits to a sufficiently negative value before softmax, not merely zero after computing probabilities without renormalization. Padding masks and causal masks address different issues. During training the full sequence is present, so the mask is essential to prevent leakage. At generation, only the visible prefix is supplied.

Transformer blocks and positional encoding

Source: book pp. 245-249 / PDF p. 273

A transformer block combines attention, a positionwise feedforward network, residual connections, and normalization. Attention mixes information across positions; the feedforward network transforms features at each position. Pre/post-normalization conventions affect optimization.

Without position information, self-attention alone is insensitive to token order in the required way. Learned or sinusoidal positional representations distinguish locations. The project embeds token identity and position together. Vocabulary size, model width, head count, depth, and context length influence parameter count, computation, and memory.

Training and analyzing GPT

Source: book pp. 250-254 / PDF p. 278

Train the vocabulary head using next-token cross-entropy. During generation, repeatedly evaluate the prefix, obtain the next-token distribution, sample under the chosen temperature, append, and stop at an end marker or length bound.

Inspect attention patterns to see which positions a head uses, while recognizing that a heat map is not a complete causal explanation. Compare held-out loss, review coherence, factual contradictions, and sample diversity. Training cost, sequential output cost, and context-length limits remain practical constraints.

Other transformers: T5, GPT-3/GPT-4, and ChatGPT

Source: book pp. 255-266 / PDF p. 283

Encoder models use bidirectional context for understanding tasks; decoder models use causal context for continuation; encoder-decoder models condition a generated sequence on an encoded input. T5 frames varied tasks in a text-to-text format and uses a denoising pretraining objective.

The book places larger GPT models in a scaling and in-context-learning context. ChatGPT-style behavior adds supervised demonstrations and preference-based training to a pretrained model. Distinguish fluent completion from instruction-following and alignment. The model descriptions are historical; undisclosed architectures or later product behavior must not be inferred from these examples.
