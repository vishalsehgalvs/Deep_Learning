Detailed Notes

Timeline: VAE/GAN, transformer, and big-model eras

Source: book pp. 391-395 / PDF p. 419

The historical sequence organizes several advances: VAEs and GANs made learned image synthesis central; transformers changed sequence modeling and transfer learning; larger models, diffusion, and cross-modal systems broadened capabilities.

These eras overlap. A newer family does not invalidate older ones, and successful systems often combine representations and objectives. Understand the underlying changes in likelihood, attention, conditioning, scale, and sampling rather than memorizing only release dates.

Large language models and text-to-code

Source: book pp. 396-401 / PDF p. 424

The chapter surveys the capabilities and limitations visible around publication: continuation, instruction following, in-context tasks, and specialized code assistance. Historical model sizes, services, and benchmarks should be read as context, not a 2026 market assessment.

Code generation connects language modeling with executable verification. A plausible program may contain incorrect logic, insecure dependencies, or invented APIs. Use tests, static checks, constrained tools, and human review proportional to risk. Product demonstrations do not establish reliable autonomous engineering.

Text-to-image and other applications

Source: book pp. 402-406 / PDF p. 430

Image generators support controlled edits, composition, and creative workflows; conditioning can include text, structure, and other media. Emerging applications extend to audio, video, 3D, scientific modeling, and interactive environments.

Separate a system’s expressive range from its consistency, controllability, and rights to use outputs. Fine detail, temporal stability, task constraints, and meaningful evaluation become more important as generators move into workflows. A useful model need not be the largest if a smaller controlled system meets the task.

Everyday life, work, and education

Source: book pp. 407-410 / PDF p. 435

The author discusses possible effects on search, communication, content creation, creative tools, work organization, and personalized learning. These are forecasts and opportunities, not proof that adoption will follow one inevitable path.

Evaluate human effort saved after verification/editing, changes to required skills, accessibility, and who benefits. Educational tools should support understanding rather than merely deliver polished answers. Human-machine collaboration, workflow design, and feedback determine whether technical capability becomes practical value.

Ethics and challenges

Source: book pp. 411-412 / PDF p. 439

Important issues include dataset consent and rights, creator attribution, bias, privacy, misleading media, concentration of capability, resource use, and misuse. Technical quality is not a substitute for social and legal judgment.

Assess potential harms before release, document data/model provenance, protect sensitive inputs, and establish review and accountability. Neither “open” nor “commercial” automatically resolves these questions. Responsible evaluation should consider people absent or underrepresented in the training data.

Final thoughts: active inference and continued learning

Source: book pp. 413-416 / PDF p. 441

The author connects generative world models with perception/action loops and the free-energy/active-inference viewpoint: an agent’s model of its environment interacts with its actions and observations. This is a conceptual perspective, not a demonstrated proof that a particular generator achieves general intelligence.

Consolidate learning by reproducing a small experiment, changing one design choice, and measuring the effect. Keep distinctions among observation, hypothesis, prediction, and evidence clear. Full references and original diagrams remain in the book for deeper study.
