Detailed Notes

DALL-E 2 architecture and text-image alignment

Source: book pp. 359-362 / PDF p. 387

DALL-E 2 separates representation alignment, a prior, and an image decoder. CLIP supplies a shared relationship between text and image representations; a prior predicts suitable image embeddings from the text condition; a diffusion decoder turns image embeddings into images.

This is not simply a language model producing RGB pixels. Distinguish the role of the text encoder, learned image-latent prior, conditional decoder, and later upsampling stages. Each can introduce failures in interpretation or rendering.

CLIP contrastive learning

Source: book pp. 362-366 / PDF p. 390

Train text and image encoders so corresponding pairs receive high similarity and mismatched pairs in the batch receive lower similarity. Normalize representations and use a temperature-scaled contrastive loss.

CLIP representations support retrieval and flexible text-based classification, but similarity does not establish compositional or factual correctness. A scene can match the general topic while getting counts, relations, or attributes wrong. Data biases and prompt wording affect alignment.

The prior, decoder, and DALL-E examples

Source: book pp. 367-376 / PDF p. 395

The prior connects text representations to the image-embedding distribution; a diffusion prior is one way to model that conditional distribution. The decoder uses image embeddings and conditioning to guide denoising, with subsequent resolution increases.

Classifier-free guidance combines conditional and unconditional predictions to strengthen adherence, typically trading diversity and sometimes artifacts against prompt alignment. Inspect text rendering, object counts, spatial relations, binding of attributes, and cultural/demographic representation. Curated examples demonstrate possibilities but do not estimate overall reliability.

Imagen and DrawBench

Source: book pp. 377-379 / PDF p. 405

Imagen conditions diffusion on a strong pretrained text encoder and uses a cascade of image-generation and super-resolution models. The book emphasizes language understanding as a major contributor to text-image alignment.

DrawBench stresses prompt categories such as composition, counting, spatial relations, unusual scenes, and text rendering. Human comparisons are useful but depend on raters, prompts, and presentation. Distinguish quality of generated imagery from correctness of what the prompt requested.

Stable Diffusion and latent diffusion

Source: book pp. 380-381 / PDF p. 408

Stable Diffusion performs denoising in a compressed latent representation rather than directly at full image resolution. An image autoencoder encodes/decodes between pixels and latents, while text conditioning guides a denoising network, commonly through cross-attention.

Compression reduces cost but can lose detail or introduce reconstruction limitations. Sampling schedules, guidance strength, resolution, and decoder quality all affect output. Available weights do not remove licensing, dataset-provenance, or misuse considerations. The described architecture refers to the book’s model generation, not every later product called diffusion.

Flamingo: vision encoder and Perceiver Resampler

Source: book pp. 381-384 / PDF p. 409

Flamingo is a visual language model that continues interleaved image/video and text inputs. A vision encoder produces visual features, while a Perceiver Resampler compresses variable-size visual input into a fixed number of tokens suitable for language conditioning.

Learned latent queries attend to visual features, controlling the amount of visual information passed downstream. This differs from treating the image only as a caption. Temporal/image organization and the model’s permitted attention over previous media matter for multi-image/video reasoning.

Flamingo language model, examples, and comparison

Source: book pp. 385-390 / PDF p. 413

New gated cross-attention components let a pretrained language model use visual tokens while retaining much of its existing capability. The original framework freezes important pretrained components and learns the connecting modules, enabling in-context visual-language tasks.

Test image-grounded answering, captioning, multi-image context, and hallucination separately. DALL-E/Imagen/Stable Diffusion generate images from conditioning; Flamingo generates language conditioned on media. Multimodality is an interface and training-design choice, not a guarantee of correct perception or reasoning.
